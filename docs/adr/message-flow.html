Transaction Message Flow

The path of one transaction, from the producer that makes it up to the row in Postgres, and every place it can leave that path. A record is stored, skipped as a duplicate, or sent to the dead-letter topic, and its offset is committed. The one exception is a failure that isn't the record's fault: then nothing is committed, the process exits, and the record is read again after the restart.

Where each record ends up

The recordWhat happensOffset
Valid and newCategorised and inserted; Postgres gives it a transaction_id (UUIDv7)committed
Already stored (a re-send or a replay)Skipped by ON CONFLICT (transaction_type, external_id, occurred_at) DO NOTHING; counted in duplicates_skipped_totalcommitted
A tombstone (no value)Skipped and countedcommitted
Bytes that won't decodeDead-lettered with reason deserialisationcommitted
Decodes, but fails validation (bad currency, a field too long, dated more than a day ahead)Dead-lettered with reason processing; the rest of the batch is insertedcommitted
Postgres rejects the row (SQLSTATE class 22 or 23)The batch rolls back and is inserted one record at a time; the bad one is dead-lettered with reason processingcommitted
A schema id the consumer didn't load at bootThe stream fails, the process exits, and the restart loads the new schema and reads the record againnot committed
Postgres is down, or any other error during the insertThe flush rejects, every flush queued behind it rejects too, the process exits, and the restart reads the batch againnot committed

So the rule is: a record that can never be stored is dead-lettered and committed, and a failure that a restart could fix is never committed.

Step by step

  1. Generate. Each source has its own producer container. avro-datagen fills the source's schema from the generator hints in it: customers and accounts come from seeded pools (5,000 customers, three accounts each), and timestamps fall in the 18 months before the anchor date. make up pins the seed and anchor, so it sends the same records every time.apps/producers/src/app.py · schemas/*.avsc
  2. Serialise. The serialiser is pinned to the schema the registry holds for <topic>-value, and never registers one itself. The bytes on the wire are a magic byte, the 4-byte schema id, then the Avro body. The schemas were registered once at startup by schemas-register, with the generator hints stripped and compatibility set to BACKWARD.apps/producers/src/producer_core.py build_serializer · infrastructure/schema-registry/register.py
  3. Produce. The key is customerId, the partition key the schema declares, so one customer's records stay in order on one partition. Each record carries a fresh traceparent and an x-producer header. The producer is idempotent with acks=all, and the record's Kafka timestamp is the time it was sent.apps/producers/src/producer_core.py produce_records · apps/producers/src/config.py
  4. Fetch. Each source has its own consumer container and consumer group. It reads from the group's committed offset, at most 1 MiB per fetch, and commits by hand.apps/consumer/src/integrations/events/kafka.ts startBatchConsumer
  5. Deserialise. The consumer loaded every registered version of its subject at boot. It reads the schema id from the record and decodes the body with that schema. Bytes that aren't in this format, or don't decode, continue as poison. A schema id it never loaded stops the consumer instead, because a newer producer is writing a version registered since boot.apps/consumer/src/integrations/events/avro-deserialiser.ts · handlers/handleDeserialisationError.ts
  6. Batch. Records collect into a batch of 100, or until 2 s after the first one, and one flush runs at a time. The consumer batching page shows this part in motion.apps/consumer/src/integrations/events/kafka.ts flushBatch, drainBatch
  7. Normalise and validate. Each source's normaliser maps its record onto one canonical transaction: the customer becomes the user, the status is lower-cased, and an EFT beneficiary's account number is cut to its last four digits. The result is checked against the canonical Zod schema, which caps field lengths, requires a three-letter currency and rejects a date more than a day ahead.apps/consumer/src/services/domain-normaliser.ts · schemas/transaction.ts
  8. Categorise. The rules loaded at boot are tried in order: merchant category code, keyword, the source's own transaction type, credit or debit for the source, then the source's default. If none match, the category is uncategorised, so this step never fails. The winning rule's version and priority are stored with the row.apps/consumer/src/services/rule-categoriser.ts · database/migrations/0001_seed_categories_and_rules.sql
  9. Insert. The whole batch goes in as one Postgres transaction, one INSERT … SELECT FROM unnest(…). Postgres assigns the transaction_id and puts the row in its month's partition. Errors are sorted by SQLSTATE: classes 22 and 23 mean a bad row, and anything else is retried by restarting.apps/consumer/src/services/transaction-ingester.ts · integrations/database/repositories/transaction-repository.ts · errors/postgres.ts
  10. Dead-letter. Records go to <topic>.dlq with acks=all. A flush's poison records go in one produce call; a bad row found during the one-at-a-time retry is sent on its own.apps/consumer/src/integrations/events/kafka.ts createDeadLetterSender
  11. Commit. Only after the inserts and the dead-letter sends does the consumer commit, at the highest offset of each partition in the batch.apps/consumer/src/integrations/events/commit.ts commitBatch

What a dead-letter record carries

Enough to send it back to its source topic unchanged once the problem is fixed: the original key, so it lands on the customer's partition again; the original bytes and headers; and headers saying why and where it came from.

// buildDeadLetterRecord, kafka.ts
topic:   transactions.card.dlq
key:     the original key (customerId)
value:   the original bytes, exactly as consumed
headers: the original headers (traceparent, x-producer), plus
  x-dlq-reason        deserialisation | processing
  x-dlq-error         the error message
  x-source-topic      transactions.card
  x-source-partition  7
  x-source-offset     184213
  x-source-timestamp  when it was produced
